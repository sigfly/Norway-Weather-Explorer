Simplified temperature-index model. Above 0 °C, melt removes snow water equivalent; remaining snow gradually densifies from fresh snow toward a maximum bulk density of 350 kg/m³. The first-hour starting depth comes from Open-Meteo's modeled snow depth where available. Temperature, sunlight, rain-on-snow, terrain and wind can produce substantial real-world differences. Changing settings recalculates the chart without downloading new weather data.

How to read these charts: “Hourly” is the amount during the preceding hour, not a point-in-time sensor reading. Snow is reported as new snowfall in cm; accumulated snowfall is the sum of new snow even if it later melts. The separate snow-on-ground chart estimates the remaining snowpack using temperature-driven melt, gradual compaction, and a modeled starting depth (where available). These are model-based historical estimates, not observations from Yr.no / MET Norway stations. These estimates are sensitive to the initial snow depth and chosen melt/settling assumptions. Large date ranges preserve hourly values in the CSV; the on-screen chart compresses points while preserving rain/snow peaks.
